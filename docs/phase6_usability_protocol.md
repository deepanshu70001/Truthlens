# TruthLens usability-evaluation protocol (not yet conducted)

## Status

No participants have evaluated TruthLens. This protocol is prepared for a future consented formative study. It contains no participant responses or usability results. Institutional/departmental ethics requirements and consent must be confirmed before recruitment.

## Study objective

Assess whether intended reviewers can use the interface and distinguish (a) image authenticity signals, (b) factual claim verification, and (c) heuristic social-context cues and unavailable data.

## Participants and consent

Recruit a convenience sample of intended academic/panel users only after local approval. Record inclusion/selection method and actual participant count. Obtain informed consent, explain that TruthLens may be wrong, explain local storage and any configured third-party data transfer, avoid sensitive personal images, permit withdrawal, and do not retain identifying participant details in public experiment artifacts. Do not imply representativeness from a convenience sample.

## Procedure (approximately 15 minutes)

1. Give a short neutral orientation with no claims about detector accuracy.
2. Ask participant to upload an approved, non-sensitive demo image and locate current pipeline status.
3. Ask them to identify authenticity result and uncertainty/caveat.
4. Ask them to distinguish authenticity from the extracted claim's fact-check status.
5. Ask them to locate evidence source/date and explain an unavailable/no-results state.
6. If Grad-CAM is available, ask what the heatmap indicates and does not establish.
7. Ask them to identify which social outputs are heuristic or unavailable.
8. Administer ratings and open-ended questions; record task completion and assistance without screen recording unless separately consented.

## Questionnaire

Use a 5-point agreement scale (1 strongly disagree, 5 strongly agree) plus “not sure/not applicable”:

- I could tell which pipeline stages completed, failed, or were unavailable.
- I could identify the image-authenticity assessment and its uncertainty.
- I understood that authenticity does not establish factual accuracy.
- I could find the evidence supporting a claim-level assessment.
- I could distinguish no evidence/unavailable retrieval from a false claim verdict.
- I understood that a heatmap is model attribution and not proof of manipulation.
- I could tell heuristic social cues apart from validated predictions.
- The report made important limitations visible.
- I would know when to seek human/source verification before acting on a result.

Open-ended: “Which result was easiest to misunderstand?”, “What evidence did you expect but could not find?”, and “What should be clearer before a public demonstration?”

## Analysis plan

Report exact participant count, recruitment, protocol deviations, task completion/assistance, per-question distributions and missing answers. Summarize open comments with a documented coding scheme; two reviewers should independently code if feasible and discuss disagreements. Do not use inferential significance tests for a small formative convenience sample. Do not claim general usability or improved trust based on this study alone.
